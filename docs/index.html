02 / Technical Topology

Three-Layer Technical Architecture (Sovereignty & Scalability)

Unified topology linking the Federated Top Level (comprising Layer A EuroHPC supercomputing & Layer B Gaia-X-aligned federated cloud), the Middle Barrier for absolute data sovereignty, and the Local Bottom Level for on-premise industrial edge nodes (Layer C).

01. Top Level: Federated Cloud & EuroHPC (Layers A & B)
ISA-95 Core Ontology (Layer 1), Sector oracles (Layer 2: HACCP, REACH, CLP) specified by the consortium and built by the teams, EuroHPC / AI Factory compute for large-model training (obtained by the teams through the EuroHPC access calls, with BSC guidance; BSC does not allocate hours), and continuous MLOps pipelines in the Gaia-X-aligned federated cloud (ESTEN).
02. Middle Barrier: Absolute Data Sovereignty
Security perimeter: signed models and knowledge packages reach the edge through plant-initiated requests or a mirror repository. During pilot validation raw plant data stays inside the factory. During development, only cleaned and anonymised datasets are shared.
03. Bottom Level: On-Premise Industrial Edge (Layer C)
Sovereign GPU edge servers inside Vicky Foods (Bakery Pilot) and Industrial Pilot 2 (candidate: Norcable / continuous process) plants. Private Knowledge Graph (Layer 3), 4-Agent Cognitive Engine with closed-loop Human-in-the-Loop operation.
03 / Operational Assistance

The AURA Edge Engine: 4-Step Closed Loop in Factory Plant

Continuous operational conversion of operator tacit experience into explicit Private Knowledge Graph intelligence across plant diagnostic workflows. The loop runs inside the plant and is fed by federated cloud services and knowledge. Data regime: during development (Stages 1–2) the pilots share cleaned, anonymised datasets for training; during pilot validation (Stage 3) raw plant data never leaves the factory.

01 · PREDICT
1. Predict
AI anticipates quality anomalies and machinery failure probabilities based on localized sensor telemetry (>2h in advance).
02 · GUIDE
2. Guide
Cognitive Engine (Dual Graph-RAG agents) provides explainable, step-by-step resolution recommendations citing official SOPs.
03 · ACT
3. Act
Human-at-the-Edge decision. Expert operator executes, modifies, or overrides recommendation, registering diagnostics.
04 · LEARN
4. Learn
Operator tacit diagnostics captured, validated by Judge Agent, and fed back dynamically into the Private Knowledge Graph.

Solution model: what each team builds

Each team delivers one complete, modular solution for one industrial challenge, built on the common AURA framework. The topic sets the competition; it does not prescribe the technical solution. This model is AURA's proposal, and teams remain free to choose their technologies.

Three different concepts

Competition
3 stages

Open call → up to 20 shortlisted → 10 funded teams → 4 finalists and grand finale. The three stages are defined by the topic; the shortlist is AURA's own filter inside Stage 1.

Industrial problem
2 challenges

A.1 Bakery (Vicky Foods) and A.2 Continuous Process (Industrial Pilot 2, to be confirmed; candidate: Norcable). Defined by the user industries.

What the solution does
4-step loop

Predict → Guide → Act → Learn. Every solution must cover all four steps.

Rules for the teams

One challenge per team

Each team chooses A.1 or A.2 and develops its solution for that challenge.

One complete solution

Each team delivers all four steps of the loop as one working solution.

Modular inside the team

A single SME can build every module, or an SME-led team can share them.

One responsible lead

The lead SME integrates the modules and answers for the complete solution.

Common framework

All teams must comply with the same interface contract, reference ontology and benchmarks, so results can be compared. The AURA reference designs (T2.3) are optional: teams choose their own technology.

Built to be reused

Plant-specific elements are configured separately, so the solution can move to other plants after the project.

Not funded: separate modules from different teams for the consortium to integrate later. Teams compete with complete solutions, as the topic identifies the best-performing solution.

What each team delivers per stage

Stage 1 · Proposal

Chosen challenge, architecture covering the four steps, and allocation of modules within the team.

Stage 2 · Prototype

Complete prototype in the sandbox, using anonymised data, tested against the benchmarks and the interface contract.

Stage 3 · Plant validation

Integrated solution validated on the production line of its challenge, with operators, up to TRL 5.

Example: how Challenge A.1 follows the 4-step loop

1 · Predict

Anticipates a quality anomaly or a failure on the bakery line.

2 · Guide

Recommends what to do and cites the official procedures.

3 · Act

The operator applies, changes or rejects the recommendation; where the line has AI-powered robots or automated equipment, the validated action can be executed by them under operator supervision.

4 · Learn

The validated experience is added to the plant's private knowledge graph.

Example challenges for the open call (illustrative)

Each pilot publishes one or two challenges (up to 4 in total), with one finalist per challenge. These examples show the kind of industrial problem a team would solve inside it, always covering the full Predict → Guide → Act → Learn loop.

A.1 Bakery · Vicky Foods
A.1.a · Baking-quality drift
Problem
Oven or proofing deviations produce under- or over-baked batches that are only detected at end-of-line inspection.
Solution
Predicts the drift before it happens, guides the operator to the right procedure, records what was done.
Data
Oven and proofing temperature and humidity, line speed, end-of-line weight and colour checks.
Target
Anomaly lead time ≥ 2 h · scrap −15%.
A.1.b · Unplanned stops in mixers, dividers and conveyors
Problem
Wear and jams cause unplanned stops; the know-how to fix them sits with a few experienced technicians.
Solution
Detects early wear signals, recommends the action from manuals and past repairs, learns from each intervention.
Data
Motor current and vibration, stop logs, maintenance records, equipment manuals.
Target
Fewer unplanned stops (baseline and target fixed in T5.1).
A.1.c · Product weight and shape drift
Problem
Weight, shape or colour drift with flour batch or dough conditions, and the response varies between shifts.
Solution
Flags the drift, suggests the adjustment citing the source procedure, captures the operator's feedback.
Data
Checkweigher, vision inspection, raw-material batch records, SOPs.
Target
Precision > 85% · 100% of recommendations traceable to source.
A.2 Continuous Process · Industrial Pilot 2 (to be confirmed; candidate: Norcable)
A.2.a · Rheology deviation of the compound
Problem
Off-spec viscosity or melt flow is found late, in lab quality control, and material or product must be reworked or scrapped.
Solution
Detects the deviation in real time and suggests a correction within the approved formulation and process limits; the operator decides.
Data
Inline and lab rheology (viscosity, melt flow), formulation, zone temperatures, screw speed and pressure, mixing times.
Target
Real-time rheology deviation detection.
A.2.b · Surface and insulation defects after extrusion, coating or curing
Problem
Bubbles, voids, surface defects or colour shifts appear after extrusion, coating or curing and lead to rejected product.
Solution
Anticipates the defect from batch and process data and recommends the preventive action.
Data
Extruder, curing and cooling zone temperatures, line speed, batch records, inline inspection and test results.
Target
Surface-defect rejects −20%.
A.2.c · Energy-aware heating and curing profiles
Problem
Heating, curing and cooling zones run conservative profiles that use more energy than the product needs.
Solution
Recommends profile adjustments that keep quality, and learns which settings work for each product.
Data
Zone temperatures, energy meters, quality-control results.
Target
Thermal energy −12% at equal quality.

Data sources and targets are to be confirmed with Vicky Foods and Industrial Pilot 2. The A.2 cases are illustrative for a continuous polymer process (compounding, extrusion, coating and curing) and will be adapted to the confirmed pilot. The final problem statements are fixed in T5.1 and published with the Stage 1 call.

Evaluation criteria per stage (proposed)

StageCriteria and weights
Stage 1 → top 10Technical excellence and fit to the challenge 35% · coverage of the 4 steps and interface contract 25% · team capacity 20% · impact and exploitation 20%. Applied twice: by the SYXIS VSI pre-screening (all eligible proposals → up to 20 shortlisted, all above the minimum threshold) and by the consortium selection committee (shortlist → top 10).
Stage 2 → top 4Technical KPIs in the sandbox 35% (automatic benchmark) · interface contract and AI Act 20% · monitoring report 15% · plant fit and deployment plan 30%, scored jointly by the two pilots for all 10 teams
Stage 3 → best solutionPlant KPIs 40% · operator acceptance (HITL) 20% · explainability and compliance 20% · exploitation plan, reusability and industry agreements 20%

Selection uses a single ranking per stage, as the topic requires; in Stage 1 the pre-screening ranking fixes the shortlist and the committee ranking fixes the top 10. Shortlist rule: only proposals above a minimum threshold are shortlisted (proposed: 60% of the total score and a minimum per criterion, fixed in the competition handbook); the 20 best-ranked of them pass, proposals tied in 20th place all pass, and if 20 or fewer eligible proposals are received there is no pre-screening beyond the threshold check. Independence: two independent external experts sit in the selection committee with a vote at every funding decision. Conflict of interest: every committee member signs a declaration of absence of conflict of interest before each selection; a partner, including a pilot, with a commercial, ownership or family link to an applicant is excluded from scoring that applicant; the pilots score only the plant-fit criterion and have no veto; the ranking is computed from the scores, and any departure from it requires a written justification agreed by the two independent experts. Plant criterion: the two pilots score the Stage 2 plant fit of all 10 teams together, not each one only the teams of its own challenge, with the same scoring grid and in a consensus session chaired by SYXIS VSI, so the ranking stays comparable across challenges. At every stage each team is scored on its attainment of its own challenge targets, relative to the T5.1 baselines, so solutions from all challenges compete on equal terms; in the grand finale the highest overall score is the best-performing solution. Finalist rule: the competition publishes up to 4 challenges, at most two per pilot (area A.1 bakery, area A.2 continuous process), fixed in T5.1 and published with the Stage 1 call. Each of the 4 finalists is the highest-ranked team of one challenge, provided it reaches all thresholds; if fewer than 4 challenges are published, or a challenge has no team above the threshold, the remaining places go to the next highest-ranked teams of the overall ranking. Selection therefore always follows the ranking, as the topic requires: each finalist is the highest-ranked team of its challenge, the same principle the topic applies to select one project per sector, and the rule is published in the competition handbook before the call opens. Each pilot commits in writing to host up to four finalists (T5.2), so the selection never depends on plant capacity, and both plants always validate.

T4.3 Evaluation methodology & stage-gate selections Lead: SYXIS VSI · M03–M34 · 17.9 PM SYXIS 12.0GRANT GARANT 1.4SINTEF 0.7UA 0.6Vicky Foods 0.6Pilot 2 0.6ESTEN 0.5INDElab 0.5KIT 0.5BSC 0.5
Implemented by the consortium as required by the topic. SYXIS VSI defines the evaluation methodology, checks conflicts of interest, chairs the consortium selection committee and consolidates the single ranking of each selection; it does not score in the committee. Stage 1, pre-screening (M10): SYXIS VSI is responsible for scoring all the eligible proposals received from GRANT GARANT and reducing them to up to 20 shortlisted proposals: those above the minimum threshold fixed in the competition handbook, capped at the 20 best-ranked, with ties in 20th place included. If 20 or fewer eligible proposals are received, only the threshold is checked. Each proposal is read twice against the grid fixed by SYXIS VSI: the first reading is done by SYXIS VSI staff and the second, independent reading is subcontracted to external evaluators (priced per proposal with a ceiling, selected on best value for money and free of conflicts of interest); where the two scores diverge beyond the threshold set in the handbook, a third external reading decides. SYXIS VSI supervises, checks consistency and decides the shortlist. If the volume exceeds the contracted ceiling, SYXIS VSI staff absorb the extra first readings and the external evaluators keep the second reading of the proposals near the cut-off. Stage 1, selection (M11): the consortium selection committee (the technical partners, each in its field, the two pilots and two independent external experts with a vote) carries out the specialised review of the shortlisted proposals and selects the top 10, which are funded in Stage 2. Stage 2 gate (M22): after the benchmark and the demo day, the committee selects the 4 finalists out of the 10 funded teams. Vicky Foods and Industrial Pilot 2 (Replacement Required), which host the solutions in their plants, score the plant-fit criterion (30% of the Stage 2 score) and have no veto; the technical criteria are scored by the technical partners and the two independent experts. The pilots score the plant fit of all 10 teams jointly, with one scoring grid and in a consensus session chaired by SYXIS VSI, so neither pilot scores only the teams of its own challenge. Grand finale (M34): the committee identifies the best-performing solution from the KPIs measured on the production lines. The pre-screening evaluators are funded from SYXIS VSI's share of the consortium core budget, and the two independent external experts of the committee are subcontracted by SYXIS VSI, never paid from FSTP; applicants cannot act as evaluators. Separation rule: external evaluators and experts have no link to applicants or partners and sign a declaration of independence; every committee member, including the pilots, signs a declaration of absence of conflict of interest and is excluded from scoring any applicant it has a commercial, ownership or family link with; GRANT GARANT does not score; no partner that mentors a team (T4.6) scores that team; benchmarks run automatically and are fixed before each stage. SYXIS VSI, represented by OhMyGrant, signs a declaration of independence, and OhMyGrant takes no part in scoring.

09 / Topic Alignment

Expected Outcomes & Draft Exploitation Plan

How AURA contributes to the three expected outcomes of the topic in the advanced manufacturing sector, and how the teams' solutions will be taken up. Expected results are pre-competitive. The call is expected to fund 3 projects under this topic, at least the best-ranked proposal in each sector (healthcare, advanced manufacturing, autonomous driving); AURA competes for the advanced manufacturing place (including AI-powered robotics) and contributes to the Apply AI Strategy (COM(2025) 723). Expected results are pre-competitive.

Technological progress
Challenge-oriented AI solutions for predictive maintenance and quality control move from TRL 2 to TRL 5, validated on real production lines against open benchmarks.
Competitiveness & visibility
The open call, the demo day and the grand finale give European AI SMEs and teams visibility with user industries, and the results reach the community through the AI-on-demand platform.
AI adoption
Explainable, human-supervised solutions are validated in the pilot plants, then reach further manufacturers through the EDIHs and the user-industry partners. Testing can use the Testing and Experimentation Facilities.

Draft Exploitation Plan: Uptake of the Teams' Solutions

Each finalist keeps ownership of its results. After the FSTP grant, the best-performing team can conclude a partnership or contract with the user-industry partners, and the other finalists are introduced to further manufacturers through the EDIHs. T6.3 develops this plan, including licensing terms, liability for recommendations and the business case for a possible post-project AURA marketplace, led by GRANT GARANT.
